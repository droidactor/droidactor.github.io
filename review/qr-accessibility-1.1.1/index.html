Dotori QR: Wi-Fi sharing accessibility review

App: com.droidactor.qr · Version 1.1.1 (4). Recorded on a Samsung SM-A6060, Android 11, on 18 November 2026, using the APK derived from the release App Bundle.

This optional service performs a fixed, user-requested action: after the user taps Share current Wi-Fi, it opens Wi-Fi settings, taps the connected network and its QR sharing button, then stops. It does not perform authentication, save the QR image, or send it. It is not an accessibility tool for people with disabilities. Wi-Fi screen information stays in device memory and is not saved, logged, or sent off the device.

The videos use the Korean interface. English descriptions below identify the actions. The other installed services, real network name and QR contents in the system sharing screen are redacted for privacy. The permission and disclosure screens are shown in full.

1. Disclosure, consent and system permission

Open the app → Create → Wi-Fi → Share current Wi-Fi → read the full in-app disclosure → Agree and open settings → Installed services → Dotori QR Wi-Fi sharing → enable → Allow → Back to the app. The app opens the connected network's QR sharing screen automatically. There are two same-named installed services on the test device; the initially disabled release service is the one enabled for this recording.

Download consent video (under 3 minutes)

2. Decline consent

Tap Cancel on the disclosure. The app stays in Create, does not open accessibility settings, and leaves the manual Wi-Fi fields available. Accessibility access is optional.

Download decline video

3. Trigger the disclosure again after declining

With the release service disabled, the manually entered synthetic open network “Dotori Demo” has a QR code. Tap Share current Wi-Fi again: the full disclosure appears again. Tap Cancel: the manual QR remains usable. No real network credentials are shown in this clip. After recording, the release service was disabled and the device's original settings were restored.
