Why 3 Is Sam, Set or Se in Korean

30 October 2026

The same written counter can have different meanings

Three minutes is 삼 분, or sam bun. Three people, when counted politely with 분, is 세 분, or se bun. The written number and counter look the same. The meaning tells you which number system to use. The app places these two expressions side by side. Each has a button that explains the reading.

SituationExpressionReading
Minutes of waiting3분삼 분 — sam bun
People, counted politely3분세 분 — se bun
The hour on a clock3시세 시 — se si
Counting animals3마리세 마리 — se mari

This is why learning two lists of numbers is only the beginning. A counter is the word used with a number to count something. Learn the number together with that word and its meaning to get an expression you can use. The comparison screen also covers other easily confused meanings. For example, 번 appears in both numbered items and repetitions.

Clock time brings both systems into one expression

Three oh three is 세 시 삼 분. The hour uses a native Korean number. The minutes use a Sino-Korean number. Open the clock view and change the hours and minutes to see the whole expression. It is a useful example when you know both number lists but hesitate when they appear together.

Native numbers also change before counters. 셋 becomes 세 in 세 마리, for example. See the result, then check its explanation to connect the form with its rule. Selected examples include dictionary-style pronunciation, IPA and romanization as reading aids.
