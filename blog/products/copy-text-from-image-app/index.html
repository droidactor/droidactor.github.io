Copy Text from Image, Block by Block

27 November 2026

https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.droidactor.textscanner

In short. Getting a few lines out of a menu, a notice or a chat screenshot usually means retyping them. The other common way is dragging selection handles across recognized text. If the lines sit in different parts of the photo, you drag several times and the order gets mixed. Dotori Text Scanner is a free text scanner for Android that copies only the blocks you tap. You copy text from image content in tap order, with no account, subscription or scan limit. I built the app myself.

Dotori Text ScannerTap the text blocks you need and copy them in order

The flow is short. Take a photo, pick one from the gallery or share one from another app, and on-device OCR starts. Each block of text gets an outline on the photo, and every block you tap gets a number. One tap on Copy puts the picked text on the clipboard in that order. Tiny blocks can be checked in the Block list, and Edit lets you fix or share the text first. Tilted photos are read again at other angles, and Rotate and pinch zoom help you inspect them.

Tap blocks on the photo and they are numbered in tap order

When text recognition finishes, each block is outlined and a block count appears below. The screenshot shows a menu photo with six blocks found and three picked. The opening hours, the dish prices and the Wi-Fi name were tapped in that order. That is why they are numbered 1, 2 and 3 regardless of where they sit on the page. Copy places them on the clipboard in exactly that order, so the pasted note needs no reordering.

To go from photo to text in four steps:

  1. Tap Take photo, or tap Gallery to pick a photo.
  2. Wait a moment while the text is read on your device and the block outlines appear.
  3. Tap the blocks you need in the order you want. Tap a picked block again to unpick it.
  4. Tap Copy to put the picked text on the system clipboard in number order.

When you unpick a block, every number after it moves up by one. Pick all and Clear picks select every block at once or let you start over. Because you pick only the text blocks you need, there is less cleanup after the scan.

Check tiny blocks in the Block list instead of tapping them

Small print at the bottom of a notice, or two tightly packed lines, can be hard to tap. Open Block list and every recognized block appears as a checklist with its text. Checking an item works exactly like tapping it on the photo. It takes the next number, and the outline on the photo updates as well. In the screenshot, the heading, the two thank-you lines and the password line are left unchecked.

The list opens as a bottom sheet and the upper part of the photo stays visible. You can compare the list against the photo while you pick. The same photo mixes a heading, prices and a Wi-Fi line. Each comes out as its own block to pick or skip.

Join lines and edit before you paste

Text in a photo often wraps to fit a narrow column. Turn on Join lines and each block becomes one line. A word split by a hyphen at a line end is joined back. On the menu, "Open daily 11:00 - 21:00" and "Closed on Mondays" were two lines. In the screenshot they now sit on one line. If line breaks matter, as in addresses or lists, turn Join lines off.

The Join lines button on the photo and the matching option in Settings share one value. Set it once and it stays for the next photo. Tap Edit to open the picked text in an editor, or all text when nothing is picked. Fix a misread character or delete a word, then copy or share it to a messenger or notes app. A character count below the editor helps when the destination field has a length limit. That way, text from the image to text converter arrives in the next app already cleaned up.

Text recognition runs on your device and retries tilted photos

Dotori Text Scanner uses ML Kit bundled in the app, so OCR runs on your phone. It reads Korean and Latin-script languages such as English. Photos and recognized text are not uploaded to a developer server and are not written to logs. A photo you take stays in the app's memory and is never saved to your gallery or a file. Closing the app or retaking a photo clears it, so copy or share what you need first.

If a photo is tilted or sideways, the app automatically tries other angles. If text is still missing, tap Rotate to turn the photo 90 degrees clockwise and read it again. On the photo, pinch with two fingers to zoom up to 8x and drag with one finger to move. Zooming does not change the recognized text, so you can compare small print with the original.

There are three ways to bring in a photo. On the camera screen, tap to focus, pinch to zoom and turn on the light in dark places. Gallery reads only the one photo you pick in the system photo picker. An image shared from a messenger, your gallery or a screenshot opens in the same flow. So the app works as an image to text tool for pictures you already have, too. The camera permission is only for taking photos; gallery and shared images work without it.

Know these limits before you rely on it

Settings cover line joining, shutter sound, font size, theme, language and screen orientation. You can turn the shutter sound off, though some devices must play it by local regulation. The app tells you when that applies. The privacy policy and advertising privacy options can also be reopened from Settings.

As with any text scanner, on-device recognition may contain errors. Check important text such as amounts, phone numbers or account numbers against the original photo. The app does not read handwriting or translate text. It does not extract text from PDF files or videos, or copy text on top of other apps' screens. Other scripts, such as Chinese or Japanese, are not recognized either.

On-device text recognition does not mean the app never uses the network. The banner ad and consent form (Google AdMob and UMP) may use it. So may ML Kit diagnostics, Play updates and operational notice checks. Photos and recognized text are not included in ad requests or notice checks.
